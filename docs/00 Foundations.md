# 00 Foundations

## Purpose

Noah's Seed is not simply a game. It is a long-term educational platform designed to grow alongside a child.

Every design decision should support flexibility, curiosity, learning, and long-term sustainability. The project should remain enjoyable for young children while being capable of expanding for many years without requiring fundamental redesign.

---

## Core Principles

### Build the engine, not just the game.

The engine defines how the world works.

The content defines what exists within that world.

The engine should never assume a particular child, family, location, activity or educational path. Those belong in configurable data.

---

### Behaviour belongs in code. Content belongs in data.

Rules, systems and interactions belong in the engine.

Names, locations, activities, educational content, families and worlds belong in data.

Changing content should rarely require changing code.

---

### Design for every family.

Noah's Seed should support many different family structures.

Examples include:

- One home
- Two homes
- Shared care
- Single parents
- Grandparents
- Foster families
- Guardians

The engine should never assume a particular household arrangement.

---

### The world grows with the child.

The world begins small and familiar.

As the child develops, the world expands naturally to include new places, ideas, activities and experiences.

The engine should support years of growth without needing to be redesigned.

---

### Curiosity drives discovery.

Children learn through curiosity.

New places, activities and knowledge should become available because they are relevant and interesting to the child—not simply because they have reached a particular age.

Curiosity should be rewarded with opportunities to explore.

---

### Readiness is more important than age.

Age may influence the complexity of educational content, but it should rarely determine what can be explored.

Instead, the engine should consider factors such as:

- Interests
- Reading ability
- Mathematical ability
- Previous exploration
- Parent or caregiver preferences

Every child develops differently.

---

### Keep the world personal.

Every child's world should feel like their own.

Homes, learning environments, activities and places should reflect the child's real life where appropriate while remaining flexible enough to support many different circumstances.

---

### Start simple.

Every new feature should begin as the simplest version that works.

Complexity should emerge gradually rather than being designed in from the beginning.

Small, well-designed systems are easier to understand, maintain and expand.

---

### Think long term.

Whenever a design decision is made, consider whether it will still make sense years from now.

Avoid shortcuts that create unnecessary limitations later.

Prefer solutions that allow the project to expand naturally.

---

## Engine Philosophy

The engine should be capable of supporting:

- Multiple homes
- Multiple caregivers
- Different learning environments
- Different educational pathways
- Different interests
- Different cultures
- Different languages
- Different abilities
- Future content that has not yet been imagined

The engine should be generic.

The content should make it personal.

---

## Guiding Question

Whenever making a design decision, ask:

> **"Will this still work for a different child, a different family, and five years from now?"**

If the answer is yes, it is probably the right direction.
