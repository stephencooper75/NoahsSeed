# Noah's Seed - Design Principles

## Purpose

Noah's Seed is a personalised adventure world designed to inspire curiosity, creativity and learning through exploration.

The primary goal is to create a world that Noah loves.

The secondary goal is to create an architecture capable of supporting personalised worlds for other children in the future.

Noah's World is the first content pack, not the limitation of the engine.

---

# Principle 1 - Systems Before Features

Every new feature should first be considered as a reusable system.

We do not build:

"Tesla feature"

We build:

"Vehicle Encounter System"

We do not build:

"Einstein character"

We build:

"Mentor System"

We do not build:

"Go-kart race"

We build:

"Challenge System"

Features are examples of systems in action.

---

# Principle 2 - Personalisation Through Data

Personal elements should never be hard-coded into the engine.

The engine should not know:

- Noah
- Archie
- Poe
- Victor Harbor
- Tesla jokes

These should exist as data.

This allows the same engine to create:

- Noah's World
- Amy's World
- Liam's World
- Public Worlds

---

# Principle 3 - Noah Comes First

The project exists because of Noah.

The first priority is creating a magical, enjoyable world specifically designed around his interests.

Commercial possibilities are secondary.

The possibility of other children benefiting should influence architecture, but should never compromise Noah's experience.

---

# Principle 4 - Learning Through Discovery

Learning should feel like part of the adventure.

The child should not feel like they are completing school worksheets.

Examples:

A road safety event becomes a lesson.

A dinosaur museum becomes biology.

An observatory becomes astronomy.

A music hall becomes music theory.

The world creates opportunities for curiosity.

---

# Principle 5 - Curiosity Before Instruction

The best learning begins with fascination.

The game should encourage:

"What is that?"

"How does that work?"

"Why does that happen?"

The answer should follow the question.

---

# Principle 6 - The World Adapts To The Child

The child should not have to adapt to the game.

The game should learn:

- interests
- abilities
- preferred activities
- learning pace

A child fascinated by astronomy receives more astronomy opportunities.

A child fascinated by trucks receives more vehicle experiences.

---

# Principle 7 - Mentors Grow With The Child

Mentors are not static characters.

They develop alongside the child.

A mentor such as Einstein may:

Age 6:
- introduce simple ideas
- encourage curiosity

Age 10:
- explore deeper concepts

Age 16:
- discuss advanced scientific ideas

The same relationship evolves over time.

---

# Principle 8 - Content Is Separate From The Engine

The engine provides capabilities.

Content creates worlds.

Examples:

Engine:
- world loading
- characters
- activities
- conversations
- saving

Content:
- locations
- mentors
- stories
- activities
- artwork

A new world should require new content, not new engine code.

---

# Principle 9 - Accessibility And Inclusion

The game should support different children.

Children may differ in:

- interests
- learning styles
- abilities
- family structures
- cultures
- languages

The system should adapt respectfully.

---

# Principle 10 - Build For The Future

We should make decisions that support long-term growth.

Before implementing a feature, ask:

1. What reusable system does this reveal?

2. Could another child benefit from this capability?

3. Will this decision still make sense years from now?

---

# Principle 11 - The World Should Feel Alive

The world should remember.

Examples:

A planted seed grows.

A mentor remembers conversations.

A pet develops personality.

A location changes over time.

The child should feel they are visiting a living world, not opening separate screens.

---

# Principle 12 - Wonder Over Optimisation

The goal is not to maximise points, levels or screen time.

The goal is to create:

- curiosity
- creativity
- confidence
- discovery
- joy

Technology serves the experience.

The experience does not serve the technology.
